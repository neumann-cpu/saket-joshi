How do you know an embedding is better?

Evaluation · representation learning

“Different” is easy. “Better” is not. Embedding and content-understanding models change constantly — new backbones, new prompts, new multimodal fusion — and offline scores often move for reasons that have nothing to do with ranking quality.

The failure mode

If you only look at retrieval recall against a fixed query set, you can reward models that collapse topics, overfit to popular items, or look strong on coarse categories while failing on the fine distinctions that matter for ranking. Pairwise preference labels help, but they inherit the biases of whoever labeled them.

Hierarchical evaluation

One approach that has worked well in practice is a topic tree: evaluate embeddings at multiple levels of semantic granularity. Coarse nodes catch catastrophic failures; fine nodes catch the distinctions that actually move ranking. Error rates that look fine at the root often look ugly two levels down.

That structure also makes ablations legible. When you swap a backbone or a prompting strategy, you can see where quality moved — not just that a single scalar changed.

Semantic-IDs

Discrete semantic identifiers on top of multimodal embeddings are useful when you need a stable, interpretable handle on content for retrieval and ranking. The modeling question is interesting; the evaluation question is more interesting: does the ID space preserve the hierarchy you care about, and does it stay stable as the underlying encoder evolves?

A practical bar

  • Prefer evaluations that fail loudly on known hard slices.
  • Report reductions in error, not only absolute scores — baselines matter.
  • Keep a path from offline win → online metric, or admit when you do not have one.
